# Problem 974

Your team is conducting skidpad testing. Telemetry shows the baseline dry cornering speed is numerically equal to double the day of the month the Formula IHU Handbook was published (in m/s). Suddenly, rain soaks the track, lowering the friction coefficient. In response, the team swaps to a lighter driver and adjusts the wings to maximise aerodynamic downforce. The data confirms that these adjustments allowed the car to match the earlier dry cornering performance exactly. Consider the car as a mass point model. Dry Baseline: The dry run was performed at the mechanical limit. Assume aerodynamic downforce is negligible for the dry configuration. The Dry Friction Coefficient (μ_dry) is numerically equal to 10% of the Handbook publication day of the month. Wet Conditions: The rain lowers the friction coefficient to μ_wet = 1.0. Aerodynamics: In the wet configuration (wings max ), the effective Downforce Area is Cl * A = 2.50 m². Environmental: Air density is ρ = 1.20 kg/m³, constant temperature. Vehicle: The empty car mass (without driver) is 230 kg. Gravity: Use g = 9.8 m/s². Calculate the Mass of the New Driver required to maintain this equilibrium (round to the nearest integer). (5 points)
